# Brief

## Working Title

What Financial Influencers Get Wrong About the S&P 500

## Subtitle Options

1. The S&P 500 is not a bad investment. But treating it as the obvious default is.
2. Buying the S&P 500 can be reasonable. Treating it as "the market" is not.
3. Simple investing advice can be useful. But when "buy the S&P 500" becomes the default answer, something important gets lost.

## Core Claim

The S&P 500 is not a bad investment, but presenting it as the default recommendation for normal private investors is hard to justify from a science-based passive investing perspective.

## Main Argument

Choosing the S&P 500 over a globally diversified ETF is an active US tilt, not a neutral default.

## Key Question

Do I have a good reason to overweight US stocks?

## Science-Based Framing

A normal private investor probably does not have an information edge that justifies overweighting US large-cap stocks over the global market.

## Analogy

Choosing the S&P 500 over a global ETF can be a bit like betting on your own country to win the World Cup. It can be emotionally understandable, but it should not be confused with the neutral baseline.

## Tone

Fair, clear, critical, not insulting. Ali Abdaal can be used as one example of a wider pattern, not as the entire focus.

## Reader Question

If you prefer the S&P 500 over a global ETF, what is your strongest reason?
