# Outline

## 1. Hook: the advice everyone hears

Open with the familiar beginner investing advice: just buy the S&P 500.

## 2. Fair concession: the S&P 500 is not stupid

Explain that the S&P 500 is cheap, simple, diversified across large US companies, and historically successful.

## 3. The hidden assumption: the S&P 500 is not "the market"

Explain that the S&P 500 is a US large-cap index, not the global stock market.

## 4. Ali / finfluencer case study

Use Ali Abdaal's investing guide as one fair example of a wider pattern: simplified advice that lowers the barrier to entry, but hides the key assumption.

## 5. The missing question

Ask: Do I have a good reason to overweight US stocks?

## 6. The uncomfortable answer: most private investors have no real edge

Explain that US excellence, US tech dominance, and strong US capital markets are widely known and likely reflected in prices.

## 7. The best counterarguments deserve to be taken seriously

List and fairly discuss:
- simplicity
- low costs
- many S&P 500 companies earn revenue globally
- US institutions and capital markets
- past US outperformance
- emotional familiarity

## 8. Why the S&P 500 still feels obvious

Discuss recent performance, English-language finance content, US tech narratives, familiarity bias, and simple advice spreading better online.

## 9. What better beginner advice would say

A globally diversified ETF is the more defensible neutral starting point. The S&P 500 can still be a conscious active tilt.

## 10. Conclusion: know what assumption you are buying

End by asking readers for their strongest counterargument.
